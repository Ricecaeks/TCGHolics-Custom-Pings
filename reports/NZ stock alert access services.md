# Hold Kmart spending; sharpen free polling first

**Run the bot on its current free sources plus Zyte for Farmers at about US$13 a month, spend nothing new before Delta Reign on 6 November 2026, and pre-approve a ceiling of about US$45 a month only if Kmart's block outlasts a multi-day clean test.** If Kmart stays blocked, the realistic range is US$30 to US$43 a month for Kmart and Farmers together, and a bounded free-credit trial should pick the vendor, starting with Oxylabs. **No service can be called reliable for Kmart NZ, Farmers or The Warehouse from published evidence:** no source tests any of them against a NZ retailer or an Akamai-protected JSON POST endpoint, so every success figure below is a proxy for the real question. Speed comes mostly from allocating polling better, because store-stock intervals, Farmers' daily sweep and retailers' own update cycles outweigh anything a vendor adds. I found no service that legitimately reveals stock a retailer has not exposed. The nearest honest answer is already in the bot's own data: Kmart's public store counts may reflect stock held before it reaches the shelf. All prices are US dollars observed on 2 November 2026 unless marked, exclude any GST, and need rechecking before purchase. Legal comments are general information, not legal advice.

## Recommended stack: US$13 now, about US$45 at most

The table is the setup to keep today. Average delay is half the polling interval and the worst case is the full interval, before any retailer feed delay.

| Retailer | Source | Interval | Average polling delay | Monthly cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kmart listings | Constructor.io | 2 min | 1 min | Free |
| Kmart store counts | Gateway, currently backing off from HTTP 429 | 15 min when answering | 7.5 min when answering | Free while it works |
| Farmers listings | Constructor.io | 2 min | 1 min | Free |
| Farmers store stock | Zyte, about 336 calls a day | Daily | About 12 h if one daily sweep | About US$13, against a US$20 cap |
| The Warehouse listings | Category pages | 2 min | 1 min | Free |
| The Warehouse stores | Member /spotted reports | Human | Variable | Free |
| JB Hi-Fi | Per-store check, no unblocker | 10 min | 5 min | Free |
| Toyworld | Per-store check, no unblocker | 15 min | 7.5 min | Free |

Two measured facts should shape the wait. The block tripped after about 2.5 hours of 5-minute sweeps, which is about **240 requests** (30 sweeps of 8). At 15-minute sweeps the bot reaches 240 requests in about 7.5 hours, so one quiet day proves little: **treat the block as cleared only after several clean days.** The second fact raises the stakes. Kmart's online flags decide online stock only, and in-store-only products surface through the store sweep (`docs/RETAILERS.md`), so the block costs more than a detail on alerts: it removes the automated signal for in-store-only restocks. /spotted is the only cover until the gateway answers again.

Delta Reign is four days away, confirmed for 6 November with over 135 cards, with no region-specific retail detail ([Pokémon](https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/the-pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-delta-reign-expansion-arrives-november-6-2026)). Do not raise Kmart's rate for it. Being blocked costs far more than a faster sweep gains. Instead, tier the free per-store checks so Delta Reign products and past sell-outs at JB Hi-Fi and Toyworld get more of the same request budget, and use the Farmers headroom deliberately. The bot throttles Farmers at 80% of its cap, which is US$16 (`docs/HOSTING.md`), leaving about US$3, or about 2,300 Zyte calls, before throttling begins. Spending most of that on Delta Reign products during release week is the only paid speed gain available without a new decision. NZ on-shelf dates are not established to match the worldwide date, so hold the raised rates for a limited window and let the bot's logs show when stock actually moved.

Faster listing polling is not the next win. Constructor's own example batches inventory every 15 minutes with 2 to 3 minutes of latency, and it advises against updates more often than every 5 minutes ([Constructor](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/integrating-with-constructor-product-catalog-catalog-ingestion-and-indexing-best-practices), [Constructor](https://docs.constructor.com/docs/integrating-with-constructor-product-catalog)). On that example, a retailer's feed averages about 10 minutes before any monitor can see a change, so moving from 2 minutes to 30 seconds saves under a minute on average. Kmart's and Farmers' real cadence is undocumented. Logging when state changes will expose any batch rhythm at no extra request cost. Discord is a small share of total delay, and its global limit is 50 requests a second ([Discord](https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/rate-limits)). The bot already sets `allowed_mentions`, which matters because webhooks parse only user mentions by default ([Discord](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/message)). Its webhook sends in `tcgpings/discord/sender.py` do not pass `wait=true`, and adding it would catch failed posts that otherwise return no error ([Discord](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/webhook)) and give a timestamp for measuring detection-to-post delay.

If Kmart stays blocked, re-decide about a week after release using logged misses and /spotted reports. Before any purchase, read Kmart's and Farmers' terms, which this research did not review, and consider asking the retailers directly for a sanctioned feed. Then run the trial at the current 15-minute cadence for about 72 hours, which is roughly 2,300 calls and 41 MB. Oxylabs' US$5 credit covers that and is the first test because per-GB billing suits 18 KB responses; its plans start at US$30 and self-service purchases are non-refundable ([Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.io/products/web-unblocker), [Oxylabs pricing](https://oxylabs.io/pricing/web-unblocker)). Nimble's free 5,000 requests a month is second ([Nimble](https://www.nimbleway.com/pricing)), and existing Zyte is the baseline at about US$3 for the same run. My suggested pass mark, a judgement and not a sourced standard, is at least 95% of calls returning valid store-count JSON with median latency under 60 seconds. Count success by checking the body, since a 200 carrying a challenge might still be billed. If Oxylabs passes on both retailers, it could replace Zyte at about US$30, provided Farmers averages under about 365 KB per response. If Zyte must stay for Farmers, the outcomes are about US$36 with Nimble or US$43 with Oxylabs, and Zyte for both is about US$42. Raise the bot's cap to about US$55 for the Zyte-only route, or Farmers will hit the 80% throttle at normal use.

**ScrapFly's caution is its 30% rule.** Failed scrapes are normally free, but if 30% or more of at least 1,000 scrapes in 24 hours are blocked without its Unblocker, failures become billable, with a 30-day recheck ([ScrapFly billing](https://scrapfly.io/docs/scrape-api/billing)). Kmart alone at 770 a day stays under that threshold, and Kmart plus Farmers at about 1,106 crosses it. The Unblocker has no fixed price because it can upgrade the proxy pool, so use `cost_budget` to cap it ([ScrapFly docs](https://scrapfly.io/docs/scrape-api/getting-started)). The bot's docs record about 30 or more credits per Akamai request, consistent with the 25-to-55 range, which fits Pro at the low end only (`docs/HOSTING.md`). The owner's good past experience is a real argument for it, but at US$100 it costs about three times Oxylabs' floor.

**Bright Data's cautions are about custom headers and POST.** Its synchronous API describes `method` as GET, its asynchronous API lists `method`, `body` and `headers` without saying POST reaches the target, and a competitor says POST and sessions are unsupported ([Bright Data API](https://docs.brightdata.com/api-reference/rest-api/unlocker/unlock-website), [async](https://docs.brightdata.com/api-reference/rest-api/unlocker/request), [Oxylabs guide](https://developers.oxylabs.io/products/web-unblocker/migration-guides/from-bright-data-web-unlocker)). Custom headers must come from a pre-approved list or pass a compliance form, and turning them on means paying for all requests, failures included. Premium domains cost more, with the rate visible only in the zone's cost panel ([Bright Data features](https://docs.brightdata.com/products/web-unlocker/features)). Its policy bars ticket bots and non-public data such as anything behind a login, and it can block domains at its discretion ([Bright Data AUP](https://brightdata.com/acceptable-use-policy)). Residential and mobile access also needs KYC, possibly with a video call and ID, though Web Unlocker does not ([Bright Data](https://brightdata.com/pricing/proxy-network/residential-proxies)).

Residential proxies are far cheaper but do less. At 0.5 to 2 GB a month, the bill is under US$16 almost anywhere. Overhead billing is unstated, so budget above the 0.42 GB of responses.

Do not choose on NZ pool size: stated figures range from 7,415 to 524,220, use different definitions, and were not independently measured. The trial order is ProxyEmpire, then Decodo or IPRoyal. A proxy changes only the IP, and it could only reset the clock if the cause is non-browser signals rather than per-IP volume. Sources selling bypass services describe Akamai scoring IP type alongside TLS, HTTP and behavioural signals, and I found no official Akamai detail ([ScrapFly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/how-to-bypass-akamai-anti-scraping)). AIMultiple also notes that residential proxies have no built-in anti-bot features ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/web-unblockers)). Rotating IPs after a retailer has begun challenging you is also a terms and legal question, covered below. Mobile is not an upgrade worth paying for: Proxyway measured 75.26% against 74.43% for residential on US targets ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/research/proxy-market-research-2026)), while a dedicated NZ line costs US$129 for 30 days ([Coronium](https://coronium.io/mobile-proxies/new-zealand)).

**Exclude NetNut outright.** The FBI and IRS-CI seized its domains on 2 July 2026 over the Popa botnet of at least 2 million compromised devices. It was widely resold and white-labelled, and Google warned that operators may rebuild by reselling other networks ([Krebs](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/07/fbi-seizes-netnut-proxy-platform-popa-botnet/)). So a clean brand name is not enough: ask any reseller, ProxyEmpire and Webshare included, which upstream networks it uses and what changed after the seizure, since Proxyway describes both as resellers ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/research/proxy-market-research-2026)). Treat very cheap traffic as a warning sign, because grey-market botnet proxies can cost US$0.15 to US$0.50/GB (same source). Consent claims cannot be checked from outside.

Commercial monitors are a benchmark rather than a replacement. DropNZ charges NZ$6 a month, says it checks every few minutes, and claims 7 seconds from Shopify restock to ping ([DropNZ](https://dropnz.com/)). Collectable charges NZ$15 and claims instant alerts across 150+ NZ stores ([Collectable.gg](https://www.collectable.gg/)). No independent speed test exists, so one month of each, at NZ$21, logged against the bot's own timestamps would be the only fair comparison.

## Nothing published proves reliability on these retailers

The best Akamai-specific evidence is one site, two years old, and a GET page: on Lowe's in September 2024, Zyte scored 100% at 1.65 seconds, Oxylabs 99.69% at 27.57 seconds, Nimble 95.95%, Bright Data 92.33% and ScraperAPI 75.84% ([Proxyway 2024](https://proxyway.com/research/comparing-popular-web-scraping-proxy-apis)). By tests run mostly in October 2025, the Lowe's average across APIs was 52.57% at 2 requests a second, with per-provider figures not read ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/research/web-scraping-api-report)). The two tests used different rates and provider sets, but old Akamai numbers should not be trusted. No independent Akamai data exists for ScrapFly, ZenRows or Scrape.do. Overall scores show how much the tester matters: Zyte is 93.14% in Proxyway's independent test ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/best/best-web-scraping-apis)), 74.75% in ZenRows' own ([ZenRows](https://www.zenrows.com/blog/best-web-scraping-apis-benchmarked)) and 68.2% in String's own ([String](https://usestring.ai/blog/web-scraping-benchmark-problem)).

**The untested assumptions are the core of this decision.** No source tests any service against Kmart's gateway, and no one documents `cpr_chlge`, so its exact meaning is unconfirmed. No vendor documents how its unblocker treats a bare API POST with no page load. The measurements record the block from the home connection and none through a paid service. Nothing shows whether the trigger is IP volume or client signals, so a vendor might work, or might work briefly. Zyte's US$0.00127 is a default in the bot's config (`tcgpings/config.py`) and not a billed rate, so the US$13 is an estimate to reconcile with Zyte's dashboard, and the assigned tier for Kmart and Farmers is unknown. Zyte's observed 30 to 83 seconds also sits well above the roughly 11-second benchmark, for reasons not established. At 83 seconds, eight Kmart calls take 11 minutes in series, so paid Kmart counts would need concurrency and could lose part of their freshness to latency. No benchmark measures a NZ exit or an 18 KB JSON response, and an AU exit might do but is untested.

Cloudflare evidence is thinner still. The notes contain no per-vendor Cloudflare success rate. Cloudflare's presence also predicts little by itself: JB Hi-Fi sits behind it and answers plain HTTP (`docs/RETAILERS.md`), while The Warehouse's store stock sits behind a human check and signed tokens. **The Warehouse store stock has no recommended price because it has no recommended route.** The owner's bot does not bypass those controls, and a vendor doing it would not change what is being done. /spotted stays the answer, ideally with reports labelled as unverified member sightings, an expiry and a second confirmation before escalating a ping. That design is my suggestion, since no restock community's rules were readable, though one app does offer confirm or deny and photo proof ([TCG Restock](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6754227978)) and DropZone also relies on members for in-store finds ([DropZone](https://dropzone.gg/)).

## No service can reveal stock a retailer hasn't exposed

**I found no scraping service that legitimately offers unpublished retailer stock.** These services fetch what a site serves publicly, while handling blocking and rendering. Zyte treats login- or paywall-protected data as outside "publicly available" ([Zyte](https://zyte.com/blog/compliant-web-scraping-with-ai/)), and Bright Data bars non-public data ([Bright Data AUP](https://brightdata.com/acceptable-use-policy)). Only a retailer can grant more, through something like a data agreement. Collectable's claim to detect a delivery "the moment it hits their system" is the closest public wording, and it names no mechanism, so it is not evidence of hidden access ([Collectable.gg](https://www.collectable.gg/)).

What looks like early stock usually has an ordinary explanation. Public surfaces update at different speeds, so a search result can lag a page that is already public, and catching that is ordinary monitoring of lag. Kmart adds a legitimate variation. Its product pages' own queries return unit counts, the bot's notes record unexplained large figures such as 286 of one product at Rotorua, and those notes say counts reflect what the system holds, which may not yet be on the shelf (`docs/RETAILERS.md`). That is a public count that may lead the shelf, not a hidden feed, and no timing data proves how much lead it gives. Comparing count changes with later /spotted reports would measure it for free once the gateway answers.

Legally, NZ has no single web-scraping statute ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.nz/articles/is-web-scraping-legal-in-new-zealand-essential-compliance-guide/)). Crimes Act s 252 covers intentional access to a computer system, direct or indirect, knowing it is unauthorised or being reckless about that, with a maximum of two years' imprisonment. I read this through excerpts only, since official pages returned errors ([NZLII](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca196182/s252.html)). Sprintlaw, a legal consultancy and not a law firm, says risk rises with bypassing technical barriers, using another person's credentials or overloading a server. My reading is that defeating a block is where this risk grows, whether or not a paid service does the fetching, and that this applies to the paid Kmart fallback in this report if it is used to get past a live challenge. I found no NZ court decision on scraping under s 252. Zyte's policy puts lawful use on the customer and bars breaching security measures. It also bars commercially exploiting Service Data for third parties, which a paid or sponsored alert service could arguably trigger ([Zyte AUP](https://www.zyte.com/terms-policies/acceptable-use-policy/)); that applies to today's Farmers route as well and is worth asking Zyte about. If the bot operates in trade, Fair Trading Act s 9 applies to its own claims, so "instant" or "first" should rest on logged figures ([Commerce Commission](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/news-and-media/news-and-events/archive/trustpower-to-publish-corrective-advertising-and-write-to-consumers-commission-also-warns-wrightson/)). Retailer terms need direct review, ideally with a lawyer if money starts changing hands.

## Conclusion

The purchase question turns out to be smaller than the speed question. A paid Kmart route buys one retailer's store counts at a 7.5-minute average, while Farmers' store stock stays on a daily sweep. Billing model also matters more than it first appears: 5-minute Kmart sweeps would be about 1.24 GB of responses, still inside Oxylabs' US$30 plan, whereas per-request vendors' Kmart bills would triple. So vendor choice depends partly on whether the owner will ever want faster store checks, and the earlier block at 5 minutes is the reason not to assume so.

The most valuable evidence is obtainable for almost nothing, and Delta Reign will generate it: which restocks were missed without Kmart's counts, whether /spotted caught them, and how far Kmart's counts lead the shelf once they return. Those logs will answer more than any vendor benchmark, and a sanctioned retailer feed, if one is ever offered, would remove the block risk that no vendor can.
